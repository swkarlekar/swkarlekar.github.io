01Additional Research Projects

Complete (or near-complete) non-archival research projects and exploratory work that I presented through posters or talks.

Examining the Structure of Text-Based Interventions in Large Language Models

Sweta Karlekar, Gabriel Chuang, and Claudia Shi · Columbia University Computer Science

Tests the linear representation hypothesis by treating concept changes in text as interventions and LLM activations as potential outcomes. Using WordNet antonyms and GPT-4-generated counterfactual sentence pairs, we compare constant shifts, linear transformations, and nonlinear mappings. Preliminary results favor constant shifts on a small sample of concepts.

Slides

Modeling the Projected Effect of Climate Change on the Prevalence of Food and Waterborne Illness Outbreaks

Nicolas Beltran and Sweta Karlekar · Columbia University Computer Science

Combines climate projections and probabilistic models within a causal-inference framework to explore food and waterborne illness outbreaks under different climate scenarios. The project compares outbreak models, examines uncertainty, and discusses limitations in data and causal assumptions.

PosterCode

Exploring a Flexible Computational Method for Comparing Massive Interaction Data from Science Visualizations

Sweta Karlekar · UNC Chapel Hill School of Education

Faculty advisor: Kelly Ryoo. Graduate student mentors: Emily Toutkoushian and Amanda Swearingen.

Developed a flexible method for comparing interaction logs from science visualizations using integer encoding and Levenshtein distance, identifying patterns across student knowledge groups to inform future personalized feedback.

02Selected Presentations and Talks

A Mechanistic Interpretability Lens on Many Shot Jailbreaking

Presented for LLM Interpretability and Alignment. Explores hypotheses about many-shot jailbreaking through in-context learning, refusal directions, and persona shifts, connecting interpretability research with proposed experiments.

“A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits” (Elhage et al., 2021)

Presented by me and Nicolas Beltran-Velez for a class on the Theoretical Foundations of LLMs, covering “A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits” (Elhage et al., 2021). Introduces the circuit view of transformers through residual streams, attention-head computations, induction heads, and circuit ablation.
