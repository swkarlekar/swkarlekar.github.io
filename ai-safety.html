AI safety & interpretability

I’m a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at Columbia University, advised by David Blei. My work focuses on understanding what language models learn, measuring their behavior reliably, and investigating how they can become safer and better aligned with human intentions.

  3. 2022–2023

    Applied ML Research Engineer · Meta

    Generative AI Safety at Meta

    I worked on AI-generated stickers in Meta’s Productionizing AI Research (PAIR) team, which led Meta’s early Generative AI efforts. As a lead engineer, I focused on making the product safer and more responsible, reducing stereotypes, and improving diversity in model outputs.

    • Used prompt expansion, fine-tuning, and large language models (LLMs) to improve representation in generated outputs.
    • In offline tests, increased gender diversity by approximately 30%, disability representation by approximately 12%, and skin tone diversity by approximately 6%.
    • Coordinated cross-functional teams on safety mitigation plans and roadmaps, led weekly syncs, tracked progress across multiple teams, and communicated results to leadership and partner teams.

  4. 2023–present

    Ph.D. research · Columbia University

    Understanding and measuring language models

    With David Blei, I study how concepts are represented in language models, how to measure them under distribution shift, and what model internals can tell us about reliability and alignment. My research connects mechanistic interpretability with probabilistic modeling and robust generalization.

    1. NeurIPS 2024

      Estimating the Hallucination Rate of Generative AI

      Develops a method for estimating hallucination rates in generative AI for in-context learning, without requiring ground-truth labels for each generated response.

      PDF

    2. Exploratory project: text-based interventions

      Tests the linear representation hypothesis by comparing how concept changes in text relate to changes in LLM activations.

    3. TACL 2026 · Presented at EMNLP 2026

      Model Directions, Not Words: Mechanistic Topic Models Using Sparse Autoencoders

      Builds topic models over interpretable sparse-autoencoder features, connecting semantic analysis with controllable text generation through topic steering.

      PDFPoster

    4. ICLR 2026 · Workshop on Unifying Concept Representation Learning (UCRL)

      Extracting Representations in LLMs Robust to Distribution Shifts

      Workshop work on CARE: extracting concept directions that remain useful for measurement and intervention across environments, including harmful-prompt detection.

      PDF

    5. NeurIPS 2026 · Accepted; forthcoming

      Do You CARE to Generalize? Extracting Robust Concept Directions from LLMs

      Introduces CARE, which separates shared concept directions from context-specific residuals. Evaluations include harmful-prompt refusal and toxicity, studying both measurement and intervention under distribution shift.

      This is not the camera-ready version; the paper will be on arXiv soon.
